The analyst's notes

Morocco arrive as the eighth-ranked side in the world and one of the form teams anywhere, unbeaten across their last ten with an African Cup of Nations run that saw them see off Zambia, Cameroon, Nigeria and Senegal, four of those wins to nil. The recent friendlies told the same story: 5-0 over Burundi, 4-0 over Madagascar, and only a 1-1 draw with Norway to interrupt the sequence. Behind it sits a €498.3m squad built around Hakimi and Aguerd in front of Bounou, the spine of a defence that has kept clean sheets almost at will.

The model likes them too, though it stops short of anointing them. It has Morocco progressing through the early rounds and reaching the semi-finals 44.6% of the time, the final 19.5% and lifting the trophy 7.9% — genuine contenders rather than favourites. Read that honestly: a one-in-thirteen title shot is the fourth or fifth tier of the field, not the top. The Atlas Lions have the form and the defensive record to trouble anyone, but the numbers say the deep run is likelier to end at the semi-final stage than in the final.

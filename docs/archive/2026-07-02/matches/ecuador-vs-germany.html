The analyst's review

Ecuador upset Germany 2-1 at MetLife, turning the form chart on its head against a side that had won nine of its last ten and carried a squad valued at €947m, more than two and a half times Ecuador's. The home side, unbeaten in their previous several outings and fresh off a qualifying win over Argentina, took the points the numbers said were least likely to land their way. The three goals cleared the over 2.5 line, an outcome the model had priced at just 32.8 percent.

Both the model and the market read this one wrong, and the market more confidently so. Polymarket had Germany at 57.5 cents to win and Ecuador at only 19.5; our model was more cautious but still leaned away at 38.9 percent against Ecuador's 29.2. The locked bracket pick — a 1-1 draw flipping to a German win — missed on both score and result. The draw was the model's most likely single outcome at 31.8 percent, so an Ecuador home win was genuinely the tail. Credit Ecuador: this was an underdog delivering against the grain.

The analyst's preview

This is a tight, low-scoring matchup on paper, and the model sees it that way: Germany are favoured but only modestly, with an away win at 38.9% against a 29.2% Ecuador win and a fat 31.8% chance of a draw. The expected goals tell the same story — 1.10 for Germany, 0.92 for Ecuador — and the scoreline distribution is bunched tightly, with 1-1, 0-1 and 0-0 all hovering around 14% as the three most likely results. Germany carry a near-threefold squad-value edge (€947m to €368.7m) and a flawless recent run, but Ecuador have been hard to break down, drawing the Netherlands, Morocco, Mexico and the USA and beating Argentina, and the model rates them defensively stout enough to keep this tense.

The disagreement is sharp where it matters. Polymarket prices Germany at 52.5 cents to win, far above the model's 38.9%, and trims the draw to just 22.5 against the model's 31.8%. The market also expects more goals: over 2.5 at 54.5 cents versus the model's 32.8%, and both-teams-to-score at 56.5 against 40.7%. So the model and the market genuinely diverge — one sees a cagey coin-flip with a live draw, the other a comfortable, open German win. Treat that as an open question.

The analyst's review

Morocco did exactly what the numbers said they would, edging Scotland 1-0 at Gillette Stadium to take the points in group C. It was a tight, low-scoring affair — no surprise given the model's projected 0.5 to 1.67 expected-goals split — and the single goal was enough for the world's eighth-ranked side, backed by a squad valued at €498m against Scotland's €170m. The clean sheet matched the picture too: Morocco were 67.8% to score first and rarely looked like conceding.

This one belongs to the model. Its single most likely outcome was a 0-1 Morocco win at 18.6%, and that is precisely what landed, so the locked bracket pick cashed. The model leaned firmly toward Morocco at 65.8%, well above Polymarket's more cautious 49.5%, and the result vindicates the bolder read — Scotland's 10.1% home chance never materialised. The market wasn't wrong to respect Scotland's recent 4-0 and 4-1 friendly wins, but it priced the draw and the upset too generously. On the day, the goal model's conviction was the better guide.

The analyst's preview

Boston gets a clean mismatch on paper: Morocco sit eighth in the world to Scotland's 43rd, and the squad valuations underline it at €498m against €170m. The model reads it that way too, handing Morocco a 1.67 expected-goals edge to Scotland's 0.5 and making them 65.8% favourites, with the draw at 24.1% and Scotland down at just 10.1%. Morocco's form backs the numbers — a run of clean sheets through their AFCON campaign, beating Senegal 3-0 and holding Nigeria — while Scotland arrive off friendly wins over Bolivia and Curaçao but with March defeats to Japan and Ivory Coast still on the sheet. The likeliest outcomes are a 0-1 (18.6%) or 0-2 (15.9%), and Morocco are 67.8% to open the scoring.

The market broadly agrees on direction but is notably warmer on Scotland: Polymarket prices the Moroccan win at 55.5 cents against the model's 65.8%, and lifts Scotland to 17.5 from 10.1. The sharper split is on both teams scoring — the model sees only a 32.4% chance, the market 45.5 — which, with over 2.5 goals also priced higher (41.5 to 36.9), suggests bettors expect a more open, higher-scoring game than the model's tight low-scoring picture. Treat that gap as a genuine open question.

The analyst's notes

Mexico arrive at a home World Cup in good order, ranked 15th and riding a run of form that has seen them lose just once in their last ten — a 2-1 friendly defeat to Paraguay back in November. Since then they have been hard to beat, blanking Portugal and holding Belgium to draws before sharpening up with wins over Iceland, Ghana and Australia, capped by a 5-1 dismantling of Serbia in early June. That is the profile of a side peaking at the right time, and as hosts they carry the schedule and the crowd with them.

The model likes the start more than the finish. It gives Mexico a 57.8% chance of winning their group and makes them comfortable favourites to clear the round of 32 at 93.9%, but the odds thin quickly thereafter: 56.7% to reach the last 16, 23.9% to make the quarter-finals, and just 0.81% to lift the trophy. With a squad valued at €191.85m, this is a team built to enjoy home advantage in the early rounds rather than to go the distance — a strong start, a steep climb beyond it.

The analyst's notes

Cape Verde arrive at their first World Cup as a side that earned the trip the hard way, ranked 69th in the world and worth a modest €54.5m. The qualifying run did the heavy lifting: a 1-0 home win over Cameroon, a 2-0 victory in Mauritius and a 3-0 dismantling of Eswatini, with only a 3-3 draw in Libya blotting the campaign. The warm-up form has been encouraging too — back-to-back 3-0 friendly wins over Serbia and Bermuda, and a 1-1 draw with Finland — though a 4-2 defeat in Chile is a reminder of the step up in class.

The model is blunt about where that leaves the Tubarões Azuis. It gives them a 31% chance of surviving the group stage into the round of 32, but only 4.2% of reaching the last 16 and 0.9% of a quarter-final. Winning the group is priced at just 1.4%, and the title at effectively zero. In short, this is a tournament about the experience and the occasion; clearing one knockout hurdle would already count as a genuine overachievement for a debutant nation.

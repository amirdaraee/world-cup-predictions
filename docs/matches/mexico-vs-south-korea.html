The analyst's review

Mexico took Group A's opener 1-0 at the Estadio Akron, a clean sheet and a single goal enough to see off South Korea in front of a home crowd in Guadalajara. It was the tighter end of the range the numbers had sketched out: just one goal scored, with neither side adding a second, in a game the model had pegged as a near coin-flip to stay under 2.5 goals (the over sat at only 45.65%). Korea's failure to score also resolved the BTTS question, which the model had priced as essentially even at 49.28%.

The model's headline read held up well. It made Mexico clear favourites at 49.7% to win, gave them a 55.81% edge to open the scoring, and listed 1-0 as its second most likely scoreline at 11.94% behind a 1-1 draw. The expected-goals split of 1.53 to 0.97 captured the shape of it neatly — Mexico the sharper attack, Korea kept quiet. The locked bracket pick called the result correctly as a home win, even if the exact 1-1 scoreline missed. A solid, honest hit.

The analyst's preview

Mexico in Guadalajara have home advantage, the better ranking (15th to 25th) and the heavier squad, and the model leans on all of it: 1.53 expected goals to South Korea's 0.97, and a 49.7% win probability against 23.4% for the visitors, with the draw at 26.9%. That fits the shape of a side that has won five of its last six, including a 5-1 dismantling of Serbia, against a Korea team capable of both 5-0 and 0-4 results in the same spring. The model sees Mexico more likely to open the scoring (55.8%) and most often lands on tight outcomes — 1-1 at 12.8%, 1-0 at 11.9% and a 0-0 still live at 8.8%.

There's barely any daylight between model and market here, which is itself worth saying: Polymarket prices Mexico at 47.5 cents, the draw at 29.5 and Korea at 23.5, all within a couple of points of the model. The one mild disagreement is on goals. The model gives over 2.5 a 45.7% chance against the market's 40.5%, so it expects marginally more action than the betting consensus. BTTS is a coin-flip both ways (49.3% to 50.5%). Treat the goal line as the genuine open question; the result odds, everyone agrees on.

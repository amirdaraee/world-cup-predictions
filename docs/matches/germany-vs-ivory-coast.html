The analyst's review

Germany got the job done at BMO Field, edging Ivory Coast 2-1 to land exactly on the home win that both the model and the market had leaned toward. The 2-1 scoreline itself was among the more plausible outcomes, the fifth most likely on the model's board at 8.95%, just behind the 1-1 it actually projected as the single likeliest result. With two goals from Germany and one in reply, the day broke over 2.5 goals, the side of a near coin-flip line the model priced at 42.76%.

The headline view comes off well. The bracket pick called a home win and got one, graded a hit. But there's an honest gap worth flagging: the model gave Germany just 46.68% to win, while Polymarket was far bolder at 62.5 cents, and the Ivorians repaid that respect by scoring and making it a one-goal game rather than the comfortable night the market implied. So a clean result for the call, a fairer reflection of the contest from the model's caution, and a reminder that an in-form Ivory Coast, winners over France this month, were never the pushover the price suggested.

The analyst's preview

Germany arrive in Toronto as clear favourites on paper — tenth in the world, a squad valued at €947m against Ivory Coast's €522m, and nine wins in their last ten, the only blemish a 2-0 qualifying loss in Slovakia. But the model sees a tighter game than the billing suggests. It projects 1.42 expected goals for Germany against 0.97 for Ivory Coast, which translates to just a 46.7% home win, with the draw at 28.1% and Ivory Coast at 25.2%. The Ivorians earn that respect: they beat France 2-1 in June and have lost only once in their own last ten. The single most likely scoreline is 1-1 at 13.3%, narrowly ahead of 1-0 Germany.

This is where the model and market part ways sharply. Polymarket prices Germany at 64.5 cents to win, far above the model's 46.7%, and trims Ivory Coast all the way to 15.5 against the model's 25.2%. The gap extends to the goals: the market has over 2.5 at 61.5 cents and both teams to score at 57.5, while the model sits at 42.8% and 47.7% respectively. In short, the market expects a comfortable, open German win; the model expects a cagier, lower-scoring contest that Ivory Coast can genuinely trouble.

The analyst's notes

England arrive ranked fourth in the world with a squad valued at €1.36bn, and the qualifying campaign backed that billing up: five straight European wins, Serbia beaten 5-0 away and 2-0 at home, Latvia thumped 5-0, and not a goal conceded across the run. The model likes them accordingly, making England 74.1% to win their group and the near-certainty of 98.6% to clear the round of 32. That is the profile of a side expected to be there in the closing stages.

The warm-up form complicates the picture. Since qualifying wrapped up England have lost 0-1 to Japan and drawn 1-1 with Uruguay at home before scraping past New Zealand 1-0, and a 1-3 friendly loss to Senegal lingers from June. So the deep-run numbers come with caveats: the model gives 50.7% to reach the quarter-finals, 31.0% the semis, but only 17.8% to make the final and 10.3% to lift the trophy. Strong enough to be among the favourites, not strong enough to be trusted blindly — the friendly stumbles are a genuine question the knockouts will answer.

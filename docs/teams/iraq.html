The analyst's notes

Iraq arrive at the World Cup having earned their place the hard way, edging Bolivia 2-1 in the intercontinental play-offs in April before a confidence-building friendly run that included a 1-0 win over Andorra and a creditable 1-1 draw away to Spain in June. Ranked 57th in the world and carrying one of the leaner squads in the field at just €21.2m, the Lions of Mesopotamia look set up to defend first: that goalless draw away to Saudi Arabia and a 0-0-flavoured cluster of tight Asian qualifiers point to a side that lives on fine margins rather than firepower.

The model is blunt about the ceiling. It gives Iraq a 13.6% chance of surviving the group into the round of 32, but only 2.3% to reach the round of 16 and effectively nothing beyond — a 0.3% semi-final shot rounds the story off. Winning the group is priced at under 1%. The recent Arab Cup, where back-to-back losses to Algeria and Jordan followed a bright start, captures the issue plainly: Iraq can compete with anyone for a half, but stringing together knockout-level performances is another matter entirely.

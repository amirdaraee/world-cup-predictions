The analyst's notes

Jordan arrive at their second straight major tournament ranked 63rd in the world, with one of the leaner squads in the field at just €20.3m. The model sees Al Nashama as long shots to advance: a 14.1% chance of reaching the round of 32, 1.6% to make the round of 16, and effectively nothing beyond the quarter-finals, where the line sits at 0.3%. Winning their group is priced at barely half a percent. None of that is harsh given the gulf in resources, but it does frame this as a campaign about survival rather than progress.

The form line tells two stories. Through the 2025 Arab Cup, Jordan looked genuinely dangerous, winning five in a row including a 3-0 at Egypt and a 1-0 at Saudi Arabia before falling 3-2 to Morocco. Since then the warm-up results have cooled the optimism: two friendly draws against Costa Rica and Nigeria, then defeats by 1-4 to Switzerland and 0-2 to Colombia. The defence that shut out three Arab Cup opponents conceded six across those two losses, and that's the question they carry into the group.
